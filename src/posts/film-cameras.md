Film cameras always seemed like a hassle on paper. Then I got an Instax Mini as a gift (a 7 or an 8, I forget which), and the photos changed my mind. They have that analogue look. To me it's pure nostalgia.

## From Instax to half frame

I started reading about film cameras. The sensible move was a cheap point and shoot. The cheapest new one I found was a Kodak KB10, at about ₹2,000. I dived too deep instead and discovered half-frame cameras.

A half-frame camera uses half of a normal frame for each photo, so a 36-exposure roll gives 72 photos. Rolls are expensive, so the idea is good on paper.

I got an Olympus Pen EES-2, imported from Japan, serviced and all. It cost ₹7,000, including shipping and the forwarding courier.

## The Olympus Pen EES-2

It's heavy and solid, but tiny in the hand. It fits in a jeans pocket.

- **Zone focus.** You guess the distance to your subject and set it on the lens. Annoying.
- **No DX coding.** The camera can't read the film speed from the canister, so I set it by hand. Fair enough.
- **All mechanical.** No batteries needed. Wow, like a watch!

## Getting film developed from Jaipur

I got respooled film from Zhenwei Labs, their [Kepler 400](https://www.lomography.com/films/871975041-kepler-400-by-zhenwei-labs-mumbai/photos), and sent my two finished rolls back to them for development. That took 7 to 10 days.

I had to, because Jaipur has no good photo labs. The shops that are left are very old and have no passion for it. They only develop the well-known Kodak films, and they charge too much.

Sending rolls is a hassle too. They have to go by land, so they aren't scanned the way air freight is. It's also the cheapest method, but only some couriers offer it.

## The first two rolls

I was very disappointed with what came back.

- The photos taken inside were dark.
- The ones taken outside missed focus.
- Many were blurred from my shaking hands. I'm used to the very good stabilisation in my Olympus mirrorless.

The camera is too clunky for me. I'm not a person who wants to deal with all that while also trying to capture my kid.

## A simpler camera

I bought another used camera, and this time a simple point and shoot. I could find very few half-frame ones, so I got a Nikon Funtouch 4 with fixed focus and a bright lens. It cost ₹4,000, cheaper than the EES-2.

- It's lightweight, and just a tad bigger than the Pen.
- It has a flash!! A very bright one.
- It takes two AA cells.
- It's DX coded, so it reads the film speed itself.
